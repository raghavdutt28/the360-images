Are your EHS/ERT teams just guessing during Emergency Evacuation?

The alarm is the easy part. Making sure each person evacuates is hard!

The alarm sounds at 3:40 p.m. People leave desks, lines, guest rooms and kitchens and walk to the assembly point. Within minutes, someone has to answer one question for the response team: is anyone still inside?

On most sites, that answer comes from a roll call: a warden with the morning register, reading names over the noise. The register lists who should be there. It doesn’t show the visitor who signed in at reception, the contractor on another sheet, or the person who left early through a side gate.

The rules are clear that the count must happen. OSHA’s emergency action plan standard requires “procedures to account for all employees after evacuation.” India’s National Building Code asks fire wardens to “perform a head count to ensure that all regular occupants known to have occupied the floor have been evacuated.” Note the word “regular”. Visitors, contractors and temporary staff are the people a register most often misses.

Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.38(c)(4) ↗︎ · NBC 2016 Part 4, Annex D-9.3.9 ↗︎

Roll call during panic incident? not a great idea.

On 30 June 2025, an explosion tore through the Sigachi Industries plant at Pashamylaram, near Hyderabad. Two days later, the district administration put the number of people on duty at 143, while a list in the plant’s security room showed 162. Eight workers were never found. Their families received death certificates about six months later.

Source: Telangana Today, 2 July 2025 ↗︎ · Deccan Herald, 29 December 2025 ↗︎

The causes of that blast are a separate matter. The lesson for every site, from an office tower to a hotel, mall or campus, is narrower: when the people on site and the names on the list don’t match, rescuers start with a guess.

Count the people live, not the names on paper.

A live headcount works the other way round. It starts with the people standing at the assembly point and compares them with who should be there. The number updates as each person arrives, so the gap is visible while there is still time to act on it.
